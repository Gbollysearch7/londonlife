# London Life balance report

Generated by `npx tsx scripts/simulate.ts` (deterministic; re-run after any balance change). All values are fictional game pounds.

## Routine modelled

- Three home-cooked meals a day (tea and toast, pasta bowl, beans on toast): £5.10 a day in groceries.
- A full shift on each workday (Monday–Friday), steady style, 3 of 4 work tasks answered well; promotions follow the real rule (100 performance, 2 shifts per grade, skill gates).
- 20 XP of free practice a day towards the next skill gate (coding club, library, home equipment), so skills never rust.
- Daily check-in every day, 2 of the 3 assigned daily missions (no all-three bonus).
- Rent, utilities and staff wages every Saturday 09:00; starting support covers rent and utilities for the first two weeks.
- Surplus cash above 2 weeks of costs goes into savings (fictional 0.5% a week).
- Bus to work when the workplace is more than a short walk; weekends: three Food Hall lunch-rush side tasks (poor and average starts only).
- Not modelled: furniture or wardrobe purchases, courses, eating out, minigame prizes, stories, treasure. These are discretionary.

## Conclusions

- Every start survives: the poorest start never drops below £100.00 and the first paid rent arrives after the two weeks of starting support.
- Careers dominate income, as intended; check-in and missions are a noticeable newcomer boost that fades in importance as pay rises.
- **Biggest balance concern: career progression is fast.** With a steady style and most tasks answered well, a promotion comes about every three shifts, so the top rank arrives around day 71–72 of weekday play; skill gates are what slow it down. Pay near the top (several thousand pounds a week) makes even the Soho townhouse affordable within about a month and the riverside penthouse within three for the fastest starts. Recommendation for the careers owner: more shifts per grade at higher ranks (for example 2/3/4/5/6 by rank) or a lower performance gain per hour. Not changed here, because it would alter the careers workstream's tested rules.
- Utilities (energy and water) are small next to rent at every tier (about 7–12% of rent) and owned homes still pay them, so ownership keeps a running cost.
- Household staff are a mid-game luxury: a cleaner and chef together cost about the same as a starter's weekly rent in a studio, and give mood and time rather than money.
- No balance values were changed by this workstream. Two items are flagged for product/economy decisions: uncapped savings interest, and selling an inherited home on day one.
